The Work.

eBay
Diagnose + Fix

The Customers Nobody Was Counting

Asked for → Complaint volume analysis
Found → A population of silently overbilled customers invisible to every existing metric

The dashboard was measuring customers who complained. Nobody asked about the ones who didn't — and were still being charged incorrectly.

Triggered a remediation program for a customer population that wasn't in any report
The ask was simple: how many complaints are we getting? Answerable in an afternoon. But while building the analysis I noticed something structurally missing — the metric was counting inbound contacts, not harm done. Customers who were overbilled but never contacted support weren't complaining. They were quietly leaving, or quietly staying and continuing to be charged. I redirected the work, built a method to identify the silent population, quantified the actual affected cohort, and brought it forward. It wasn't in my job description. It wasn't what they asked for. But once you see it, you can't unsee it.
eBay
Deliver + Diagnose + Fix

Shipping Label Fraud — Before Anyone Asked

Asked for → Routine shipping data analysis
Found → A coordinated fraud ring operating at scale inside the platform

A pattern in the data didn't match any legitimate commerce behavior. Nobody had flagged it. Nobody was looking for it.

Multi-million dollar fraud ring surfaced. Federal law enforcement involvement.
Standard shipping analysis. I was looking at label generation patterns when I noticed a cluster of accounts with weight distributions too consistent to be real commerce. The regularity was the tell — legitimate sellers have variance. These didn't. I built a detection model on nights and weekends, not because it was assigned, but because the pattern was too clear to ignore. What started as a data curiosity became a formal investigation with FBI involvement. I found the thing by doing the work, not by being asked to look for it.
Upwork
Diagnose + Fix

19 Weasel Words on One Slide

Asked for → Weekly business review support
Found → A measurement system structurally designed to avoid accountability

The WBR was full of language that sounded like progress without committing to any measurement. I counted the phrases. Then I rebuilt the system.

WBR redesigned around actuals vs. targets — language that couldn't obscure the numbers
I audited a sample of WBR slides and catalogued what I started calling weasel words — "trending in the right direction," "we believe," "early signals suggest," "momentum is building." Nineteen instances on one slide. Every phrase allowed a presenter to claim progress without committing to a measurement that could prove them wrong. The language wasn't the problem. It was a symptom of a system that didn't require truth-telling. I documented the pattern with evidence, made the case to leadership, and rebuilt the reporting framework so slides showed actuals vs. targets, not narratives.
Amazon
Deliver + Diagnose + Fix

The Gen Z Lens Nobody Had Built

Asked for → Standard behavioral reporting
Found → A generational shift in platform engagement that existing metrics were structurally blind to

The WBR tracked categories and transactions. It wasn't built to see how a generation was engaging differently — and the difference mattered.

New cohort-based tracking framework became a standing leadership artifact
The existing reporting was accurate. It was also blind to a cohort-level behavioral shift that would only become a strategic problem once it was too late to address. I proposed a generational lens — a structured way to track how Gen Z was engaging with the platform differently from older segments — not because it was assigned, but because the gap was obvious once you looked at the data without the legacy framing. What started as a one-off proposal became a recurring leadership review. The metric existed. The framing to make it meaningful didn't.
